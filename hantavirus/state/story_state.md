# Hantavirus Cruise Ship Story State
*Last updated: 2026-05-11 (Cycle 6)*

---

## Summary

An outbreak of Andes virus hantavirus (ANDV) has occurred aboard the MV Hondius, a Dutch-flagged expedition cruise ship operated by Oceanwide Expeditions. As of May 11, 2026 (Cycle 5): **at least 10 confirmed/probable cases, 3 deaths** (CFR ~30%). Ship disembarked Tenerife May 10; **MV Hondius now en route to Rotterdam** for decontamination, with 61 crew and one body aboard. Post-disembarkation spread confirmed: French passenger (Paris) and first American (Nebraska) both confirmed. Second American mild-symptoms case still unresolved. WHO DON601 still not published. CDC situation page not yet updated.

### The Virus
- **Strain**: Andes virus (ANDV) — confirmed by WHO on May 6, 2026
- **Significance**: ANDV is the **only hantavirus strain known to transmit human-to-human**, via close and prolonged contact (not casual exposure)
- **Typical incubation**: 1–6 weeks (4–42 days) — WHO DG explicitly noted more cases could emerge given incubation window
- **Fatality rate**: ~38% among those developing respiratory symptoms (Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome)
- **Treatment**: No specific antiviral; supportive care; ECMO can improve survival to ~80% if started early
- **Reservoir**: Long-tailed pygmy rice rat (Oligoryzomys longicaudatus) in southern South America; **no natural ANDV reservoir in Europe**
- **Source**: WHO, CDC, ECDC

### Index Case & Origin
- **Leading theory** (Argentine health officials, per AP): Dutch male passenger contracted ANDV during **birdwatching activities in Ushuaia** before boarding on April 1
- He had completed a 4-month road trip (Nov 27, 2025 – Apr 1, 2026) through Chile, Uruguay, and Argentina
- **Argentine authorities are now testing rodents along his route** (Cycle 2 update)
- **Status**: "Leading theory" — not yet formally confirmed
- **Source**: Wikipedia, WHO DON599, Argentine health ministry (via AP), Euronews

### Transmission Aboard
- Initial acquisition: environmental (rodent exposure in Argentina, pre-boarding)
- Subsequent: human-to-human transmission aboard ship confirmed (close-quarters living)
- WHO working hypothesis: index case infected secondary cases via close personal contact
- **Source**: WHO DON600, ECDC assessment

### Cases by Nationality (known)
- **Deaths**: Dutch (x2 confirmed), German (x1)
- **Cases**: Dutch, German, Swiss, South African, British, **French (1 confirmed May 11)**, **American (1 confirmed PCR-positive May 11)**
- Full breakdown not yet published by WHO
- **Source**: Wikipedia, WHO DON600, Euronews, French Health Minister (May 11), HHS (May 11)

### Response Actions
- WHO experts boarded ship; WHO DG Tedros traveled to Tenerife to oversee evacuation
- WHO classified as **Level 3 emergency response**
- Medical evacuations by air ambulance during voyage (Netherlands, Germany, Switzerland, South Africa)
- **Saint Helena (~30–40 passengers disembarked April 24)**: All identified and contact-traced across 12 countries as of May 7; no confirmed secondary cases reported
- ECDC deployed EU Health Task Force; EU Reference Laboratory offering diagnostics; 2,500 diagnostic kits shipped from Argentina to 5 countries
- CDC team at Tenerife, 0 of 17 US nationals tested positive at Tenerife; **no mandatory quarantine upon US arrival**
- 17 US nationals + 1 British/US resident repatriated to **Nebraska Medicine/UNMC National Quarantine Unit** (Offutt AFB); arrived Omaha morning May 11
- **One American PCR positive** on arrival: transferred to Nebraska Biocontainment Unit (Level 1 Special Pathogen Treatment Center); asymptomatic
- **Second American with mild symptoms**: traveled in aircraft biocontainment, undergoing clinical assessment
- State health departments being notified of returned passengers
- **14 Spanish nationals**: 45-day quarantine at Hospital Central de la Defensa Gómez Ulla, Madrid
- **5 French nationals**: Evacuated to Paris May 10; one confirmed positive May 11 (condition worsening, in specialist infectious diseases hospital); 22 French contact cases identified and isolating
- **Source**: CDC, WHO, NBC News, ABC News, Euronews, CBS News, NL Times, French Health Minister Rist (May 11), HHS (May 11)
