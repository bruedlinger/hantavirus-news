### The Virus
- **Strain**: Andes virus (ANDV) — confirmed by WHO on May 6, 2026
- **Significance**: ANDV is the **only hantavirus strain known to transmit human-to-human**, via close and prolonged contact (not casual exposure)
- **Typical incubation**: 1–6 weeks (4–42 days) — WHO DG explicitly noted more cases could emerge given incubation window
- **Fatality rate**: ~38% among those developing respiratory symptoms (Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome)
- **Treatment**: No specific antiviral; supportive care; ECMO can improve survival to ~80% if started early
- **Reservoir**: Long-tailed pygmy rice rat (Oligoryzomys longicaudatus) in southern South America; **no natural ANDV reservoir in Europe**
- **Source**: WHO, CDC, ECDC

### Index Case & Origin
- **Leading theory** (Argentine health officials, per AP): Dutch male passenger contracted ANDV during **birdwatching activities in Ushuaia** before boarding on April 1
- He had completed a 4-month road trip (Nov 27, 2025 – Apr 1, 2026) through Chile, Uruguay, and Argentina
- **Argentine authorities are now testing rodents along his route** (Cycle 2 update)
- **Status**: "Leading theory" — not yet formally confirmed
- **Source**: Wikipedia, WHO DON599, Argentine health ministry (via AP), Euronews

### Transmission Aboard
- Initial acquisition: environmental (rodent exposure in Argentina, pre-boarding)
- Subsequent: human-to-human transmission aboard ship confirmed (close-quarters living)
- WHO working hypothesis: index case infected secondary cases via close personal contact
- **Source**: WHO DON600, ECDC assessment
