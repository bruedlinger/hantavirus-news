Today's Alerts — May 18, 2026

► Alert American physician confirmed Ebola-positive; U.S. imposes first-ever Ebola travel ban May 18, 2026 at 8:21 PM UTC  ·  delivery pending (notify.sh not configured)

What changed

  • Dr. Peter Stafford confirmed Ebola-positive. An American physician working at a hospital in Bunia, DRC since 2023 tested positive for Bundibugyo ebolavirus. He is being transported to Germany for treatment — the first confirmed Ebola case headed for European soil in this outbreak. His four children and two colleagues (including his wife, who had patient exposure) are also being relocated; colleagues under observation/testing. (STAT News, May 18)
  • U.S. imposes first-ever Ebola travel ban. The Trump administration has banned foreign nationals from DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan who have been in those countries within the past 21 days from entering the US. Signed by Jay Bhattacharya (NIH director/acting CDC). Exempts US citizens, military, and DHS-approved travelers. Duration: 30 days. South Sudan included despite no confirmed cases. Defies WHO guidance against travel restrictions. (STAT News, May 18)
  • Africa CDC weighing continental security emergency (PHECS). Africa CDC is considering declaring a Public Health Emergency of Continental Security — an escalation beyond the WHO PHEIC under the AU governance framework. Africa CDC DG cancelled WHO World Health Assembly meetings in Geneva to return to Africa. AU Chair (Burundi President Ndayishimiye) engaged for political guidance. (Africa CDC Statement)
  • WHO coordination framework visibly fracturing. US travel ban + Rwanda's earlier border closure = two actors defying WHO's explicit “no travel/trade restrictions” guidance within 24 hours of each other.

Why this matters

Dr. Stafford's confirmed infection meets two independent alert criteria simultaneously: a named healthcare worker positive, and an Ebola patient being transferred to Germany — potentially the first case on European soil. The US travel ban is unprecedented for any Ebola outbreak and signals a break from WHO-coordinated response. If Africa CDC declares a PHECS, that adds a second emergency tier to what is already a first-ever PHEIC-without-Emergency-Committee.

  • Africa CDC PHECS declaration (watch africacdc.org)
