# Ebola Story State — 2026 Ituri Province (Bundibugyo) Outbreak

*Last updated: 2026-05-18T13:55:00Z — Cycle 3*

---

## Summary

A Bundibugyo ebolavirus (BDBV) outbreak began in DRC's Ituri Province in late April 2026, with initial spread to Uganda's capital Kampala and a confirmed case in Goma (North Kivu, under M23 control). WHO declared a PHEIC on May 17, 2026 — notably without convening an Emergency Committee, which is unprecedented (EC will be convened retrospectively per IHR). There are no approved vaccines or treatments for the Bundibugyo strain (existing licensed vaccines target Zaire, which is ~40% genetically different). This is the **LARGEST documented Bundibugyo outbreak ever** (previous record: 131 cases, Uganda 2007). As of May 17, 336 suspected cases and 89 deaths, with 12 lab-confirmed cases — no new count as of 13:55 UTC May 18. Rwanda closed ALL DRC border crossings (Rubavu-Goma and Rusizi) as of May 17, directly contradicting WHO guidance against border closures. This is DRC's 17th Ebola outbreak since 1976.

## Confirmed Facts (with sources + dates)

### Core Epidemiology

- **Virus:** Bundibugyo ebolavirus (BDBV) / *Orthoebolavirus bundibugyoense* — confirmed via PCR and genomic sequencing by INRB (Institut National de Recherche Biomédicale)
- **CFR:** Historical 30–50% for Bundibugyo (2007 and 2012 outbreaks)
- **Incubation period:** 2–21 days
- **Reservoir:** Fruit bats suspected (zoonotic origin)
- **No approved vaccines or treatments** for Bundibugyo strain; two WHO-prequalified vaccines (rVSV-ZEBOV, Ad26.ZEBOV/MVA-BN-Filo) target only Zaire strain; Bundibugyo is ~40% genetically different from Zaire — no cross-protection
- **Scale record:** LARGEST documented Bundibugyo outbreak ever. Previous BDBV outbreaks: 2007 Uganda (131 cases), 2012 DRC (38 cases). At 336 suspected cases this massively exceeds prior scale. (Imperial College Q&A, May 2026)
- **Detection gap — deeper than official figure:** Imperial College epidemiologists note the volume of community deaths before official declaration suggests the outbreak "likely went undetected and spread for several weeks or even months" before April 24 symptom onset in first identified case — the actual origin may predate the official April 24 index case

### Geography

**DRC — Primary:**
- Ituri Province: Bunia, Rwampara, Mongbwalu health zones (8 confirmed cases as of May 15)
- Goma, North Kivu: 1 confirmed case (wife of Bunia victim traveled to Goma while infected; area under M23 Movement control — response access uncertain)

**Uganda — Cross-border:**
- Kampala: 2 confirmed cases (both imported from DRC)
  - First: 59-year-old Congolese male, admitted Kibuli Muslim Hospital May 11, died May 14
  - Second: confirmed May 16, no local transmission identified at time of report

**DRC — Ruled out:**
- Kinshasa: initial case report DISPROVEN — confirmatory tests were negative (WHO confirmed). Wikipedia as of May 18 still lists Kinshasa as affected — treat as Wikipedia lag; WHO ruling is authoritative.

**Not yet affected (monitored):**
- South Sudan: no confirmed cases; US Embassy Juba alert issued May 17; included in Africa CDC coordination meeting
- Rwanda: no confirmed cases. **Border closure CONFIRMED** — Rwanda closed ALL crossings with DRC (Rubavu-Goma AND Rusizi entry points) effective May 17, "for an indefinite period" per Rubavu District Mayor Prosper Mulindwa. Exceptions only for nationals returning home under mandatory testing + quarantine. Daily cross-border water-fetching by DRC residents now prohibited. WHO explicitly advised against border closures — Rwanda is defying this guidance. (KT Press, Xinhua, May 2026)
- Burundi: no confirmed cases; no reporting
- CAR: no reporting

### Patient Zero / Timeline

- **April 24, 2026:** First suspected case (nurse, Bunia) developed symptoms; died within 3 days (may not be true index case — see detection gap note above)
- **May 5, 2026:** WHO alerted; experts deployed to Ituri
- **May 11, 2026:** First Uganda case admitted to Kibuli Muslim Hospital, Kampala
- **May 14, 2026:** Uganda Case 1 dies; INRB confirms first positive PCR tests for Bundibugyo
- **May 15, 2026:** DRC officially declares outbreak; 246 suspected/80 deaths; Africa CDC emergency coordination meeting with 30+ partners
- **May 16, 2026:** Uganda Case 2 confirmed; 3 health zones confirmed (Bunia, Rwampara, Mongbwalu); WHO DG signals PHEIC
- **May 17, 2026:** WHO declares PHEIC (without Emergency Committee — unprecedented); 336 suspected/89 deaths/12 confirmed; Goma case confirmed; Kinshasa case ruled out; US Embassy Juba issues alert; US begins coordinating evacuation of 6 exposed Americans
- **May 18, 2026:** US Embassy Kampala issues new health alert (content unavailable); no updated case count as of 09:43 UTC

### PHEIC Declaration — Notable Details

- Declared May 17, 2026 by WHO DG Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus
- **Unprecedented:** First PHEIC declared without Emergency Committee recommendation — International Panel for Pandemic Preparedness called this a reflection of "the gravity of the situation"
- WHO explicitly recommends: **No country should close its borders or restrict travel/trade**
- Does NOT meet criteria for "pandemic emergency"

### Demographics / Transmission Pattern

- Majority affected: age 20–39 years
- >60% female — suggests "significant risks associated with household and caregiver transmission"
- Amplified in healthcare settings (inadequate IPC protocols)
- Enhanced by unsafe burial practices
- 3-week gap between first suspected death (April 24) and official confirmation (May 15) — WHO noted "low clinical index of suspicion among healthcare providers"

### Contact Tracing (as of May 15)

- 65 contacts listed; 15 identified as high-risk
- Follow-up hampered by insecurity and movement restrictions
- Several listed contacts died before isolation (tracing gap)

### Security Context

- **Ituri Province:** ADF (Allied Democratic Forces) active — attacks limiting health worker access and disrupting contact tracing in the primary outbreak zone
- **Goma / North Kivu:** M23 Movement controls the area where the confirmed Goma case is located — response access uncertain
- Both armed groups separately confirmed to hamper outbreak response (CNN, Al Jazeera, CIDRAP reporting)
- Mining-related population movement in Rwampara and Bunia amplifies spread
- Refugee displacement from regional conflicts

### Response Actions

- WHO rapid response teams deployed to Ituri since May 5
- Africa CDC: Urgent coordination meeting May 15 with 30+ partners including WHO, UNICEF, FAO, US CDC, ECDC, China CDC, pharma companies (Gilead, Merck, J&J, Regeneron, Roche, Abbott), Gates Foundation, Wellcome Trust
- **MSF:** Two Ebola transit centres in Ituri (Bwanasura and Bunia); 3 suspected cases isolated at Salama clinic, Bunia; large-scale response mobilization in progress; teams assessing medical needs (MSF, May 2026)
- **US evacuation:** SIX exposed Americans being relocated under US government coordination; options include evacuation to US or US military base in Germany; at least one may have developed symptoms; testing pending (STAT News, May 17)
- US Embassy health alerts: Kampala (May 15 + May 18), Kinshasa (May 15), Juba/South Sudan (May 17)
- CDC Travel Advisories: DRC = Level 2 (Ituri Province preexisting Level 4); Uganda = Level 1
- Exit screening at airports and border crossings for febrile illness
- **USAID structural gap:** USAID dismantling has weakened contact tracing capacity, border screening infrastructure, and laboratory capacity in the region — structural deficit vs. prior DRC Ebola responses (Gounder, May 2026)
- **PPE stockpile warning (Africa CDC):** If transmission escalates, PPE stockpiles would require 6–8 weeks to replenish — potential supply chain bottleneck in a surge scenario (Health Policy Watch, May 2026)
- **Rwanda border closure (CONFIRMED):** Rwanda's Rubavu District Mayor Prosper Mulindwa confirmed closure of Rubavu-Goma and Rusizi crossings effective May 17, "indefinite." Exceptions for nationals returning under quarantine; daily cross-border water-fetching now prohibited. WHO guidelines explicitly oppose border closures — Rwanda defying this guidance. (KT Press + Xinhua, May 17–18 2026)
- **EC retrospective convening:** WHO will convene Emergency Committee "as soon as possible" after the PHEIC declaration — the EC will advise on temporary recommendations retrospectively, not in advance as normally required under IHR. (Health Policy Watch, May 2026)
- **Mongbwalu as likely origin:** Mongbwalu mining zone identified as probable site of outbreak origin — consistent with mining-linked population movement amplifying spread. (Health Policy Watch, May 2026)
- **American evacuees update:** 3 of 6 at high-risk contact; 1 showing symptoms; no confirmed positive tests as of May 18 13:55 UTC; CDC coordinating withdrawal (CBS News, May 2026)

### Treatment & Vaccine Pipeline

- **No approved countermeasures** for Bundibugyo ebolavirus (Bundibugyo ~40% genetically different from Zaire — no cross-protection from licensed vaccines Ervebo/Inmazeb/Ebanga)
- **RCT candidates (4 under assessment):** Monoclonal antibodies, Gilead remdesivir (IV), oral remdesivir analog, plus one unspecified fourth candidate — being assessed for randomized controlled trial protocols in DRC and Uganda
- **Oxford/Moderna broad-spectrum vaccine:** In early development; CEPI + EU funded (~$26.7M); targets Bundibugyo, Zaire, Sudan, and Marburg; not near clinical readiness for this outbreak
- **Sabin/IAVI:** Sudan ebolavirus vaccines in clinical trials — not applicable to Bundibugyo
- Timeline for trial initiation in-country: unknown (open question)

### Background: DRC Ebola History

- This is DRC's **17th Ebola outbreak** since virus identification in 1976
- Most recent prior outbreak: declared over December 1, 2025 (64 cases, 45 deaths — different strain, Zaire)
