# DIGEST May 18, 2026 — Ebola Bundibugyo: PHEIC declared, American physician infected, first-ever US travel ban

2026-05-19T11:20:00Z — cycle 5 (first run of May 19, covering May 18 developments)

## What changed

- **WHO declared PHEIC** — May 17 declaration (without Emergency Committee — first time in WHO history); does not meet "pandemic emergency" criteria but is a formal international emergency designation.
- **Dr. Peter Stafford confirmed Ebola-positive** — American physician at Nyankunde Hospital, Bunia; first US national confirmed with Bundibugyo. Transported to Germany for treatment; 6 additional US contacts also transferred to Germany for monitoring. German hospital undisclosed.
- **US imposes first-ever Ebola travel ban (Title 42)** — Foreign nationals from DRC, Uganda, South Sudan barred from US entry for 30 days if present in those countries within past 21 days. South Sudan included preventively (no confirmed cases). Both US and Rwanda now openly defying WHO "no travel restrictions" guidance.
- **Africa CDC formally declared PHECS** — May 18, after prior WHO PHEIC; first-ever PHECS for an Ebola outbreak. Emergency Consultative Group chaired by Prof. Salim Abdool Karim recommended the declaration. $2M mobilized; joint IMST with WHO activated under "4 Ones" coordination model.
- **Case count escalated significantly** — From 246/80 (May 15) to 336/89 (May 17) to ~395/106 at PHECS declaration. Three-week detection delay explained by incorrect lab testing cartridges (samples initially tested for Zaire, not Bundibugyo).
- **Rwanda confirmed all DRC border crossings closed** — Primary source: Rubavu District Mayor Mulindwa; effective May 17, indefinitely; daily water-fetching by DRC residents prohibited.
- **PPE stockpile warning** — Africa CDC: 6–8 week replenishment window if transmission escalates. Mongbwalu mining zone identified as probable outbreak origin.

## Why this matters

May 18 was the most consequential day of the outbreak so far — a named American healthcare worker infected, two independent emergency declarations (PHEIC + PHECS), and the first US travel ban for Ebola. The detection delay being explained by wrong lab cartridges suggests the surveillance gap was partly systemic/procedural, not just access-related.

- WHO Emergency Committee convening date and temporary recommendations
- Stafford colleagues' test results; German hospital case registration
